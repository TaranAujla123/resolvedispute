The whole situation

Everyone sees their part of the problem. Few see all of it.

Lawyers see legal issues. Accountants see financial ones. Brokers see transactions. Each professional is looking at their particular corner of the problem — and each is right about that corner.

But complex situations rarely live in one corner. There is the legal exposure, yes, but also the money, the timing, the people, the incentives, the relationships, the pressure each side is under, and what tomorrow looks like. Sometimes what you actually need is one person to look at the entire situation, honestly, from outside it — and help you decide what to do.

The one rule that matters

Advisor, not neutral.

Mediation
I’m the neutral, on no one’s side, helping two parties reach their own agreement.
Advisory
I’m on your side, helping you think, decide, and move. One point of view: yours.
The firewall

Because those two roles can’t mix, I won’t later mediate a dispute I’ve privately advised one side on, and I won’t privately advise one side to a dispute I’m mediating. Advisory is also not legal advice — if a matter needs legal interpretation, or becomes an actual real estate transaction, I’ll tell you plainly, and it moves to the right professional or through the brokerage.
