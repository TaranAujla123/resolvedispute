What is usually underneath it

Rarely the house. In most of these files it is some combination of:

  • Who cared for the parent in the last two years, and whether anyone has ever said so out loud.
  • A promise made verbally, and remembered three different ways.
  • The sibling who is estate trustee being treated as though they are stealing, when they are drowning in work nobody else wanted.
  • Someone who needs the money now and someone who does not.
  • And underneath all of it: this is the last thing this family will ever divide.

A court can order the property sold. It cannot do anything about any of the above, and the process will make most of it worse.

The two roads

Estate litigation

Slow, and expensive in a way that is particularly painful here — the legal costs often come out of the estate, which means every side is paying for all of it.

The trustee is personally exposed and behaves defensively, which makes them look worse, which escalates it further.

A public court record.

Whatever is left of the family afterward.

A mediated resolution

The contents dealt with before the money, because the furniture fight is about who is remembered and it will poison everything else if left underneath.

The trustee’s two roles separated out loud — the person and the office — so criticism of the administration stops reading as an attack on a sibling.

One jointly instructed appraiser instead of three competing opinions.

Buyout, sale, or one beneficiary taking the property against their share, with the numbers actually worked out.

Where this needs a lawyer and not me

Refer out

Questions about whether the will is valid, whether the person had capacity, whether anyone was unduly influenced, dependant support claims, and a contested passing of accounts are estate litigation. I do not do any of that, and a file where those are live needs counsel before it needs a mediator.

What I can help with is the part underneath: several people, one property, and a decision that has to be made.
