Separation · Ontario

We are separating. What happens to the house?

It is usually the largest thing either of you owns, and the decision has to be made while everything else is also happening. This page is about the property question only — which is one part of something much bigger that belongs with your own lawyer.

Read this part first

Where I stop

A matrimonial home in Ontario has a special status that does not attach to other property, and how family property is divided, what support is payable, and how those interact are family law questions. You each need your own lawyer for that, and you should get one before you agree to anything.

I do not advise on entitlement, I do not draft separation agreements, and I do not tell either of you what you would get.

And if there is any suggestion of fear, control or coercion in your situation, this is not the right process and I will say so. That is not something to manage with a better technique.

What I can actually help with

The narrow, practical question, with both of you independently advised: what happens to the property.

  • Sell now, sell later, or one of you buys the other out.
  • What it is genuinely worth, rather than what either of you hopes.
  • What a buyout actually requires — and whether the lender will release the other person from the mortgage, which is the step that quietly kills most of them.
  • Who lives there in the meantime, who pays what, and for how long.
  • Timing, including whether a school year or a market matters here.
